## Overview

4Responsible.AI is designed for a future in which artificial intelligence systems become increasingly capable, autonomous, adaptive, and general.

The framework does not depend on a specific definition or arrival date for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

Instead, it addresses a workforce and organizational question that becomes increasingly important as AI capability grows:

> How can organizations increase artificial intelligence capability without unintentionally diminishing human capability, judgment, authority, learning, skill, and agency?

This question applies to today's generative AI, emerging agentic AI systems, and future systems that may approach or satisfy definitions of AGI.

---

# Why AGI Changes the Responsible AI Question

Traditional Responsible AI approaches often focus on issues such as:

* safety;
* fairness;
* transparency;
* privacy;
* security;
* reliability;
* accountability;
* and technical alignment.

These questions remain essential.

However, increasingly capable AI creates an additional responsibility:

**What happens to human capability as machine capability increases?**

An AI system may be safe, accurate, compliant, and technically aligned while still contributing to:

* declining human judgment;
* reduced problem-solving practice;
* skill atrophy;
* excessive cognitive dependence;
* weakened institutional knowledge;
* poorly bounded delegation;
* reduced human authority;
* or diminished capacity to challenge automated decisions.

4Responsible.AI addresses this human-side adoption problem.

---

# From AI Alignment to Human Agency

AGI discussions frequently focus on aligning artificial intelligence with human goals and values.

4Responsible.AI introduces a complementary concern:

> Humans must retain sufficient agency and capability to meaningfully establish, evaluate, challenge, and revise those goals.

Human Agency therefore extends beyond simply having a person present in an AI workflow.

A human may technically remain "in the loop" while lacking:

* sufficient expertise to evaluate the system;
* authority to override its output;
* practical knowledge of the underlying task;
* confidence to challenge its recommendation;
* or meaningful decision rights.

For this reason, 4Responsible.AI distinguishes between:

**Human-in-the-Loop**

and

**Human Agency**

The objective is not merely to keep humans in the loop.

**The objective is to keep humans capable of leading, bounding, evaluating, challenging, and evolving within the loop.**

---

# The AGI-Era Human Agency Problem

As artificial intelligence systems become more capable, organizations may naturally delegate larger portions of cognition and work to machines.

This creates a potential asymmetry:

**AI Capability ↑↑
Human Capability ↓**

The machine improves while the humans responsible for directing, evaluating, governing, and correcting it practice fewer of the activities required to maintain their own competence.

4Responsible.AI refers to this potential degradation as **Atrophy Risk**.

Atrophy Risk becomes particularly significant when humans delegate activities involving:

* reasoning;
* synthesis;
* judgment;
* planning;
* decision-making;
* creativity;
* problem formulation;
* communication;
* interpretation;
* and institutional memory.

A central proposition of 4Responsible.AI is therefore:

> Greater AI capability should increase the requirement for intentional human capability preservation, not decrease it.

# The Four Principles in an AGI Context

## Propel

Organizations should use increasingly capable AI to improve learning, intelligence, innovation, productivity, and outcomes.

AGI-level capability should not automatically imply unrestricted automation.

Propel asks:

**Where can increased AI capability create meaningful human and organizational advantage?**

---

## Protect

Organizations should protect the human capabilities necessary to direct, evaluate, challenge, govern, and responsibly use increasingly capable systems.

Protect asks:

**Which human cognitive and social capabilities remain strategically necessary even when AI can perform the underlying task?**

---

## Preserve

Organizations should identify activities whose removal from human work could produce harmful skill or judgment atrophy.

Preserve asks:

**What should humans continue to practice, understand, experience, and decide?**

Preservation does not require preventing automation.

It requires intentionally identifying the capabilities organizations cannot afford to lose.

---

## Bound

Increasingly capable AI requires increasingly explicit authority boundaries.

Bound asks:

**What may AI inform, think about, learn from, plan, decide, and execute?**

It also asks:

**Under what triggers, constraints, budgets, permissions, escalation rules, and human decision rights may those activities occur?**

---

# Agentic Delegation and AGI

The Agentic Delegation Process provides a structured way to evaluate authority across six levels:

**Inform → Think → Learn → Plan → Decide → Execute**

As AI capability increases, the technical ability of a system to move through these levels may increase rapidly.

4Responsible.AI argues that:

> Technical capability does not automatically create legitimate authority.

An AI system being capable of deciding or executing an activity does not, by itself, determine whether the organization should delegate that authority.

Delegation should remain an intentional organizational design decision.

---

# Human Coding in an AGI Environment

Human Coding is the process of intentionally designing human and AI activities into jobs, workflows, and organizational systems.

In a highly capable AI environment, Human Coding asks organizations to identify:

* activities that should remain human-led;
* activities appropriate for AI augmentation;
* activities appropriate for AI delegation;
* human skills that should continue to be practiced;
* decision rights that should remain human;
* and capabilities whose loss would create organizational vulnerability.

Human Coding therefore shifts workforce design from:

**What can AI automate?**

to:

**What should AI perform, what should humans continue to perform, and what human capabilities must remain encoded in the organization?**

---

# PACE and Cognitive Preservation

PACE stands for:

**Practice → Augment → Compare → Encode**

PACE provides a human capability-development mechanism for increasingly AI-enabled work.

### Practice

The human first engages with the activity sufficiently to develop or maintain relevant capability.

### Augment

AI is introduced to extend human cognition, productivity, or learning.

### Compare

The human compares their reasoning, assumptions, decisions, or output with the AI-supported result.

### Encode

Important human capabilities are intentionally retained inside future workflows.

In an AGI context, PACE provides one approach for preventing increased system capability from becoming increased human dependency.

# AI Capability and Human Capability

A central 4Responsible.AI measurement principle is to evaluate both sides of adoption.

## AI Capability Gained

Organizations may measure improvements in:

* speed;
* quality;
* scale;
* intelligence;
* automation;
* learning velocity;
* productivity;
* and innovation.

## Human Capability Preserved or Developed

Organizations should also evaluate:

* critical thinking;
* judgment;
* reasoning;
* creativity;
* problem formulation;
* communication;
* collaboration;
* empathy;
* decision-making;
* learning;
* and adaptive capacity.

The success condition is not simply:

**AI Capability ↑**

The desired condition is:

**AI Capability ↑
Human Capability ↑
Human–AI Interaction Quality ↑
Human Agency Preserved ↑**

---

# The Coevolutionary Workforce

4Responsible.AI describes the desired future state as a **Coevolutionary Workforce**.

A Coevolutionary Workforce does not treat humans as static resources operating alongside increasingly intelligent machines.

Instead, both sides of the system are intentionally developed.

The organization asks:

* How is AI capability improving?
* How is human capability improving?
* How is interaction between the two improving?
* What capabilities are humans losing?
* What authority is being delegated?
* What human capabilities should remain strategically preserved?
* What new human capabilities are required because AI has become more capable?

This produces a different future-of-work objective.

Rather than:

**Automate as much work as possible**

the objective becomes:

**Engineer the most capable combined human–AI system while intentionally preserving meaningful human agency.**

---

# Relationship to AGI Safety

4Responsible.AI should not be interpreted as a replacement for AGI safety, technical alignment, cybersecurity, governance, evaluation, or regulation.

It provides a complementary organizational and workforce perspective.

AGI safety asks questions such as:

**How do we ensure highly capable AI remains safe and aligned?**

4Responsible.AI adds:

**How do we ensure the humans and institutions interacting with increasingly capable AI remain capable, authoritative, adaptive, and agentic?**

Both questions may become increasingly important as AI capability grows.

---

# A Human Agency Architecture for Increasingly Capable AI

4Responsible.AI can therefore be understood as a Human Agency Architecture that spans a progression of AI capability.

### Generative AI

Primary focus:

augmentation, learning, comparison, AI literacy, and human skill preservation.

### Agentic AI

Primary focus:

delegation, authority, decision rights, orchestration, execution, and Human Coding.

### Increasingly General AI / AGI

Primary focus:

human agency, institutional cognition, bounded authority, meaningful control, capability preservation, adaptation, and human–AI coevolution.

The architecture is designed to remain relevant even as the specific capabilities of artificial intelligence systems change.

---

# Core Proposition

The long-term proposition of 4Responsible.AI is:

> The responsible development of increasingly capable artificial intelligence must be accompanied by the responsible development and preservation of human capability.

A future in which machine intelligence compounds while human capability erodes may be technologically advanced while remaining organizationally fragile.

4Responsible.AI proposes a different objective:

**AI capability should compound alongside human capability, human judgment, human learning, and human agency.**

That is the foundation of the Coevolutionary Workforce.
